The DIWY Pricing Model®: A Different Approach to Paying for Financial Advice

By Al Faber, CFP®, ChFC®, CLU®

Published: December 1, 2026 | Estimated reading time: 10 minutes

Email

Aligning advisor incentives with client interests through transparency, efficiency, and flexibility.

The Challenge with Traditional Pricing Models

How financial advisors are compensated can influence the services they provide, the incentives they face, and what clients ultimately pay.

Several compensation models are commonly used, each with advantages and potential conflicts of interest.

Assets Under Management (AUM): Advisors typically charge a percentage of the assets they manage. While this provides predictable revenue and may include ongoing investment management and financial planning, fees generally increase with portfolio size, regardless of the time or effort required. A client with twice as many assets may pay twice as much without receiving twice the service or value.

Flat Fees: Clients know what they'll pay in advance, providing cost certainty. However, flat fees can create competing incentives. Clients may want to maximize the services they receive, while advisors have a financial incentive to minimize the time required to deliver them.

Traditional Hourly Fees: Charging for time creates a more direct relationship between services and cost. However, advisors may have less financial incentive to work efficiently, particularly when preparation, research, and administrative activities are billed separately. Clients may also hesitate to ask questions or request assistance because every interaction potentially increases their bill.

None of these models is inherently inappropriate. Each can work well depending on the advisor, client, and services provided. The question is whether a different approach could better align the interests of both parties.

Introducing the DIWY Pricing Model®

The DIWY Pricing Model® (Do-It-With-You) is an alternative approach to hourly financial planning fees built around four principles:

  1. Pay for time together. Clients generally pay only for time spent meeting directly with their advisor. Preparation, research, analysis, and administrative work are incorporated into the hourly rate rather than billed separately.
  2. Pay after receiving service. Fees are billed in arrears, based on actual meeting time, rather than collected in advance.
  3. Reward focused engagements. Hourly rates decline as additional time is spent with the client within a rolling 30-day engagement window, reflecting the efficiencies of concentrated work.
  4. Maintain flexibility. Advisors establish their own rates and discount schedules, while clients determine how much assistance they want or need.

Together, these principles are intended to create a more transparent, flexible, and efficient relationship between clients and advisors.

1. Pay for Time Together, Not Behind the Scenes

Under traditional hourly billing, clients may be charged for meetings, research, preparation, analysis, and other work performed outside their presence. While that work can be valuable, clients often have limited visibility into how much time it actually requires.

The DIWY Pricing Model® takes a different approach: Clients generally pay only for the time they spend meeting with their advisor.

This doesn't mean the advisor performs no work outside meetings. Preparation, research, analysis, and follow-up are often essential to providing quality financial advice. The difference is that the advisor incorporates the expected cost of these responsibilities into the hourly meeting rate rather than billing for them separately.

This creates an incentive for advisors to develop efficient processes, improve their expertise, and use technology effectively. Clients aren't separately paying for an advisor's learning curve or inefficient workflow.

It also makes fees easier to understand. A client who meets with an advisor for two hours knows the amount of billable time without having to evaluate a detailed accounting of behind-the-scenes activities.

Advisors should clearly disclose how they treat brief emails, phone calls, and other communications. Routine administrative correspondence might be included without additional charge, while substantive consultations may count as billable meeting time. The important principle is that clients understand what constitutes billable time before services are provided.

The objective isn't to eliminate preparation or research. It's to encourage advisors to perform that work efficiently while remaining accountable for the quality of their advice.

2. Building Trust Through Shared Accountability

Many financial planning engagements require payment before services are delivered. In effect, the advisor is asking the client to pay first and trust that the advice will be worth the cost.

The DIWY Pricing Model® reverses that arrangement: The advisor provides the service first, and the client pays afterward.

Engagements are organized into rolling 30-day billing windows, beginning with the first meeting rather than the start of a calendar month.

For example, if a client begins an engagement on November 10, the initial 30-day window runs through December 9. The client is billed at the end of that window based on actual meeting time.

If additional assistance is needed after the window closes, a new 30-day window begins with the next meeting, and the hourly rate schedule resets. There is no requirement to schedule a minimum number of meetings or maintain an ongoing engagement.

Clients aren't paying for meetings that haven't occurred, and scheduling changes don't leave them with prepaid services they may struggle to use. While this shifts short-term credit risk to the advisor, it demonstrates confidence in the value of the advice delivered.

The arrangement also creates a more balanced relationship. Clients evaluate whether an advisor is competent and trustworthy, while advisors evaluate whether a prospective client is financially capable of paying and whether the relationship is likely to be productive. Both parties have an opportunity to assess the relationship before making a commitment.

3. Recognize the Efficiency of Focused Engagements

Financial planning often becomes more efficient when meetings are concentrated within a relatively short period.

During an initial meeting, an advisor must become familiar with the client's circumstances, goals, and concerns. Subsequent meetings can build on that foundation, reducing the need to revisit earlier discussions or become reacquainted with the client's situation.

The DIWY Pricing Model® recognizes this efficiency through declining hourly rates within each 30-day engagement window.

For example, an advisor might establish the following schedule:

Meeting time within 30 daysHourly rate
First hour$500
Second hour$400
Third hour$350
Fourth and subsequent hours$300

Under this hypothetical schedule, a client receiving four hours of advice would pay $1,550 rather than $2,000 at a constant $500 hourly rate.

The discount reflects the efficiencies of a focused engagement while still compensating the advisor for additional time.

There is no prescribed discount schedule. Advisors determine their own rates, the size of each reduction, and the minimum hourly rate at which additional discounts stop.

The model also doesn't dictate meeting frequency or duration. Four hours of advice could occur during one extended meeting or across several shorter meetings within the same window.

Declining rates don't entirely eliminate the incentive to extend meetings. Advisors still have a responsibility to keep discussions focused, productive, and appropriate to the client's needs.

The objective is to reward efficiency without restricting how clients and advisors choose to work together.

4. Allow Complexity and Client Needs to Determine Cost

Not every financial planning engagement requires the same level of expertise or effort.

A straightforward question about retirement account contributions may require less preparation than a complex analysis involving stock options, business ownership, taxes, and estate planning.

The DIWY Pricing Model® accommodates these differences in two ways.

First, advisors can establish hourly rates that reflect the complexity of the services provided, including the expected preparation, research, and analysis. The applicable rates and discount schedule should be disclosed and agreed upon before the engagement begins.

Second, the amount of meeting time can adjust to the client's needs. Someone seeking a second opinion may need only one meeting, while another client working through multiple financial planning decisions may benefit from several meetings over multiple engagement windows.

Clients can receive as much or as little assistance as they need, without committing to a large upfront planning fee or an ongoing asset-based charge.

Managing the Advisor's Risks

Like any compensation structure, the DIWY Pricing Model® involves trade-offs. Two practical concerns for advisors are nonpayment and scope creep.

Nonpayment: Billing in arrears means the advisor accepts some credit risk. However, financial advisors are in a somewhat unique position to evaluate that risk. Understanding a client's financial circumstances is already an integral part of the planning process, often providing insight into their ability to pay for services.

Financial circumstances don't guarantee payment, but they can provide useful context when evaluating the relationship. The advisor's financial exposure can be further managed through clear engagement terms, defined billing periods, timely invoicing, and policies requiring outstanding balances to be resolved before additional services are provided.

Scope creep: Meeting-based billing doesn't create an obligation to provide unlimited research, analysis, or deliverables. Advisors and clients should agree on the scope of services and establish reasonable expectations. If the client's needs expand beyond the original scope, the advisor can recommend additional meeting time or a subsequent engagement window. Any additional services and their associated fees should be discussed and agreed upon in advance, without introducing separate charges for behind-the-scenes work.

Advisors also need to price their services appropriately. Because preparation and research aren't billed separately, hourly rates must account for the work reasonably expected outside client meetings.

The model is designed to reward efficiency, not to encourage advisors to shortcut necessary analysis or compromise the quality of their advice.

Who Might Benefit from This Approach?

The DIWY Pricing Model® may be particularly attractive to clients who want professional guidance while retaining control over their financial decisions.

Examples include self-directed investors seeking a second opinion, individuals approaching retirement, employees evaluating equity compensation, and households navigating significant financial decisions.

It can also accommodate clients who want a comprehensive financial assessment followed by targeted assistance with specific planning needs.

Other compensation models may be more appropriate for clients who prefer to delegate ongoing investment management or want a broader package of continuously available services.

The objective isn't to suggest that one model works for everyone, but to offer another option for clients and advisors who value transparency, flexibility, and direct engagement.

A Framework, Not a Prescribed Fee Schedule

The DIWY Pricing Model® isn't intended to prescribe what financial advisors should charge. Rather, it establishes a framework for how fees can be structured to better align advisor incentives with client interests.

Advisors retain flexibility in setting their rates based on their experience, expertise, operating costs, and the complexity of the services they provide.

What remains consistent is the underlying approach: meeting-based billing, payment in arrears, rolling 30-day engagement windows, and declining hourly rates that recognize the efficiencies of focused work.

The model doesn't eliminate every potential conflict of interest. No compensation structure can. Advisors must still exercise professional judgment, communicate clearly about the scope of their services, and deliver advice that serves their clients' interests.

A Better Alignment of Interests

The goal of the DIWY Pricing Model® is straightforward: Create a pricing structure in which advisors are rewarded for providing efficient, high-quality advice, while clients pay for the professional guidance they actually receive.

By combining meeting-based hourly billing, payment in arrears, declining hourly rates, and flexible engagements, the model attempts to address several limitations of traditional financial advisor compensation.

For clients, it offers greater transparency and control over costs. For advisors, it provides a framework that rewards preparation, efficiency, and productive client relationships.

Ultimately, the best pricing model is one that supports a relationship in which both the client and advisor believe they're receiving fair value.
